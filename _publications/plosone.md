Small cats are far more specialised than their larger relatives in how they use habitat, yet they are rarely surveyed — which makes it hard to say whether their populations are secure. This study used camera-trap captures from a central Indian tiger reserve to estimate the abundance of two widespread species, the jungle cat and the rusty-spotted cat, and to ask what separates them on the landscape.

The answer was habitat, and it was consistent: forest cover and evapotranspiration were positively associated with rusty-spotted cat occurrence, while both factors had a significant negative relationship with jungle cat occurrence. The two species segregate — the rusty-spotted cat in well-forested terrain, the jungle cat in open scrubland.

That division matters for management. A reserve managed for one small cat will not automatically hold the other, and density estimates of this kind give conservation planning something firmer than presence records to work with.
